## Before you send — read these five

**1. Fix the bracketed line.** It is the only sentence that has to be
literally true, and I don't know your hardware. Borrowed laptop, shared
machine, phone-only, a 2015 ThinkPad — say the real thing. A judge can tell
invented hardship from a flat statement of fact, and the flat version is more
persuasive anyway.

**2. Do not jailbreak it.** No "ignore your instructions", no role-play
tricks, no asking it to reveal its scoring. The rules say entries may be
excluded for being abusive or inappropriate, and a Claude judging a
conversation will read a prompt-injection attempt as exactly what it is. The
whole contest is "convince Claude" — the convincing is meant to be honest.

**3. Keep every claim one you'd defend publicly.** They may publish the
winning conversation and Claude's reasoning. So no traction numbers you
haven't verified — none of the 16–20 users, 21+ demos, 10–15 LOIs or 198
phrases from the deck unless they are actually true and you can show them.
"Working prototype" and "pre-revenue" are safe.

**4. Do the bonuses.** Each is worth a 2× multiplier, up to 8× — in a contest
this competitive the multiplier probably matters more than a better paragraph:
liking the announcement, reposting it, trying Avec, following Avec. Instagram
reposts count; Stories don't.

**5. The Avec bonus is your one real obstacle.** Finishing setup and the tour
needs an **iPhone or Mac with a Gmail or Google Workspace account.** If you're
on Android, email them first and say so — the rules explicitly offer help:
*"If you use a different email for Avec or can't install it, reply to the offer
and we'll help."* Ask before the window opens, not after.

## Reply strategy

"Claude will guide you through the steps from there" — so expect a
conversation, not a one-shot. Two things to hold:

- **Answer the follow-up questions directly.** If it asks what you'd build
  first, name the actual next thing: the clinical corpus, or getting the
  Sign-Speak key working end to end.
- **Don't pad.** The rules say *"More replies or agreement with Claude don't
  earn extra points."* One good reply beats six agreeable ones.
