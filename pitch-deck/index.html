Unmute04

The obstacle

General sign models fail exactly where clinical communication lives.

Vocabulary

Trained on broadcast and lecture footage. Knows family, school, weather.

Has never seen hypertension, NPO after midnight, or a fingerspelled drug name.

Numeracy

Fifteen milligrams and fifty milligrams differ by one handshape.

Dosage is the highest-consequence token in the room.

Grammar

ASL has its own syntax and non-manual markers.

Anything that transcribes word-for-word produces signed English, which Deaf users correctly reject.

Unmute05

The wedge

Two hundred signs and phrases carry most of the routine clinical risk.

Pain

Location, severity, onset, duration

History

Allergies, current medication, prior conditions

Consent

Affirmation, teach-back, understanding check

Discharge

Dose, frequency, warning signs, follow-up

Bounded vocabulary. High stakes. Repeated thousands of times a day, in every hospital in the country.

v1 ships

Not in v1

  • Text → sign synthesis

  • EHR integration
  • Offline mode

Appendix A3

Regulatory posture.

The ADA requires hospitals to provide effective communication, including a qualified interpreter where necessary for meaningful access. Unmute sits alongside that obligation, addressing the routine encounters where no interpreter was ever booked.

Unmute does not satisfy the requirement for a qualified interpreter and is not positioned as doing so. Consent for procedures, diagnosis disclosure, mental health assessment and end-of-life discussions remain human-interpreter conversations. This positioning must be reviewed by US healthcare counsel before external use.

The same discipline applies to data: HIPAA posture, a signed BAA, and a security review are M3 deliverables, not assumptions.

Appendix A4

Data ethics and consent.

How the corpus is collected

  • Consented, paid Deaf participants and qualified interpreters
  • Recorded for vocabulary, not for patient encounters
  • Annotated to a protocol owned by an ASL linguistics advisor
  • Regional variation preserved, not averaged away

What we will not do

  • Train on patient data from pilots
  • Accept terms that grant a vendor rights to our recordings
  • Scrape existing sign-language dictionaries
  • Publish a model we cannot explain the data provenance of
Highest legal exposure in the venture — and the slide most likely to be probedA4

Appendix A6

Why not Nigeria first?

ASL was chosen because the published clinical evidence, the open datasets, and the payer with budget all exist there. Nigerian Sign Language is the founder’s home context and a genuine second-market ambition.

NSL is not a current capability. ASL and NSL are distinct languages with different grammar and vocabularies. Nothing in this deck, and nothing in the v1 product, applies to Nigerian Sign Language without a separate corpus and validation.
