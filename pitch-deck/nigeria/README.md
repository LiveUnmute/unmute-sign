## Why these six and not the other twenty-one

The fundraising deck argues that a US clinical ASL corpus is a defensible asset.
That argument needs a $250k round and a payer with budget, and it is not what
this room is for.

The summit is NITDA, the Ministry of Health, state hospitals, and Nigerian
investors. What they can act on is a product in front of them, a language they
recognise, and asks small enough to say yes to in a corridor. So: the ward, the
product, the honest status, the wedge, three asks. No market-size slide, no TAM,
no round.

## Rules that were kept

1. **Slide 4 carries as much as slide 3.** The "does not exist" column names the
   absent model, the absent accuracy number, the absent pilot, and revenue. It is
   what makes the other five slides believable.
2. **No claim of training an NSL model.** The line is licensed engines plus an
   owned clinical layer — the vocabulary, the refusal behaviour, the record.
3. **No accuracy number.** There isn't one. Inventing it here would be the single
   fastest way to lose the room.
4. **The ask is two departments and two advisers**, not funding. Money is
   mentioned once, at the bottom of slide 6, and deliberately played down.

## If it is shown on a projector

Slide 4 is the one to slow down on. Read the right-hand column aloud — a founder
who lists what he does not have gets listened to for the rest of the slot.
