What works today, and what doesn't.

You are looking at a working prototype, not a finished product. This page tells you exactly which parts are real, so you never have to guess whether something is broken or simply not built yet.

Reading the deployment configuration.

The honest split

Three lists. Nothing is exaggerated in any of them.

Real, working now

  • Two-way interface — patient signs on one screen, clinician speaks on the other
  • Camera capture with an explicit consent gate before anything is filmed
  • Patient confirmation — the transcript is shown to the patient and only spoken once they agree
  • Speech recognition for the clinician, using the browser's own engine
  • Repeat control on the clinician screen

Depends on the engine

  • Sign recognition — active only when a key is configured; the status banner above says which state you are in
  • Signed replies — the avatar appears only if the engine returns a video
  • With no key, the patient's phrases come from a fixed script

Not built yet

  • Clinical vocabulary — the recogniser is general ASL, not hospital ASL
  • Continuous signing — short takes only; a long flowing sentence will fragment
  • Saved history — nothing is stored between sessions
  • Live interpreter hand-off, BAA, audit logging

Try this

About five minutes. Chrome, Edge or Safari — Firefox cannot capture video here.

  1. Open the app and tap Speak to patient.
  2. Tap the speech button and say “Where does it hurt?” — then watch the patient's screen. If an engine is connected, the reply is rendered as signing. If not, you get a still pose and a note saying so.
  3. Now the other direction. Tap the hand button. Read the consent line — it names who receives the video — then tap Patient agrees.
  4. Sign something short: one or two signs, not a sentence. Tap the hand again to stop.
  5. Read the transcript. It shows a match percentage. If it is wrong, tap No — sign again and confirm that nothing was spoken.
The confirmation step is the product. At the accuracy these engines actually deliver, roughly one sentence in ten is wrong. On a ward, a wrong sentence is a clinical event — so nothing reaches the clinician that the patient has not read and approved first.

Where it will disappoint you

Better to read this here than to discover it during a demo.

Accuracy

  • General-purpose models, so medical terms will fail: milligrams, NPO, penicillin
  • Fingerspelling and names are unreliable
  • Poor lighting or hands out of frame produce confident nonsense, not an error

Flow

  • One 8-second take at a time — this is a phrase reader, not a flowing conversation
  • Each take needs a tap to start and a tap to stop
  • There is a delay between stopping and reading — usually a second or two

What happens to the video

The part that matters for a patient.

Where it goes

  • The clip is sent to the translation provider named on the consent screen
  • It is not written to storage by this app — no recording, no history, no upload of anything else
  • The camera is stopped, not just hidden, when you leave the screen

What is still missing

  • A signed business associate agreement
  • A written no-training-on-our-data clause
  • A stated retention period and sub-processor list
Do not use this with a real patient yet. Testing with colleagues, or with a Deaf signer who has agreed to be recorded, is the point of this build. A patient in a bed is not — not until the agreements above exist in writing.
