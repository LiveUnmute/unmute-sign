## The five findings that matter most

1. **The pitch is two stages ahead of the evidence.** There is no demo, no repository, no accuracy figure, no pilot, and no signed partner anywhere in the corpus.
2. **The deck contradicts the research on the central claim.** "0 interpreters needed" (deck) vs "complement rather than replace interpreters" (research doc) — 15 days apart.
3. **The NSL capability claim is unsupported by its own roadmap.** The deck says NSL is supported; the same deck says the NSL dataset does not exist and must be built.
4. **The only traction metric is unauditable.** Seven payments, with no recorded amount, date, channel, or consideration.
5. **No Deaf person holds decision-making authority.** A hearing solo founder, no Deaf advisor, a pitch built on a deceased Deaf woman's story, and a claim that AI makes interpreters redundant.

---

## The five risks that matter most

1. Technical — the stated spec (bidirectional, <2s, offline, avatar, mobile) is not credibly achievable on the current evidence.
2. Language transfer — ASL validation tells you almost nothing about Nigerian Sign Language.
3. Dataset consent and ownership — undefined, and the highest legal exposure in the venture.
4. Community legitimacy — the pattern of claims risks reading as extraction rather than partnership.
5. Institutional procurement — the assumed hospital buyer may not exist in the form modelled.
